TITLE: Homecoming
FROM: Zora
TO: Her family
PLACE: On the road to Prague
DATE: Midsummer 1541

Dearest Father, Mother and little Eliska,

We are three days from the city gate, and I cannot hold the pen still. Wenzel says my handwriting has left the page entirely and is flying in circles over the road.

Oh, there is so much to tell you. We saw the sea at Genoa. We ate a fish in Lyon that Wenzel swears looked back at him. In Paris a stranger stopped us in the street to tell Wenzel that he had the face of a man who had survived something, and he answered, quite gravely, that he had survived a great deal of soup. He laughs now. He sleeps through the night. He has stopped reaching for the sword when the door opens. I think I have done something good, whatever else I have done.

Mother, I have a Venetian mirror for the hall, and a Florentine lamp that makes no smoke, and for Father a little book of alchemical figures that I do not believe he has seen. Eliska, yours is a surprise and you may not guess it.

The coachman says the sun has been strangely pale for a week. He says the whole country says so. I find I do not mind. I am going home, and I am going home married, and I could not be happier if it never rose again.

Wait for us on the bridge.

Your Zora
