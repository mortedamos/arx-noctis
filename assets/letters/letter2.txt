TITLE: The House on the Hill
FROM: Zora
TO: Wenzel
PLACE: Prague
DATE: Autumn 1537

Wenzel,

Your three letters came together, tied with a bit of hay, and I laughed at the soup until Mother asked what was wrong with me. Nothing is wrong with me. Write again. Write when you are tired. I do not need it to be clever.

I will tell you a thing I cannot say aloud in this house. Father and my uncles have stopped sleeping. They say the Great Work is nearly done, the blackening and the whitening behind them, and that they only wait on the last stage. There is a new lock on the cellar, and a furnace beneath it that has not gone cold in a year. Men in grey coats come at night and do not give their names. Father says he is close to a thing that will end decay forever. He says it as one says grace.

Come home, and bring all your fingers. I would like to be married by someone who still has his hands.

Your Zora
