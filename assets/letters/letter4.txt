TITLE: The Newlyweds
FROM: Sergeant Niklas
TO: Wenzel and Zora
PLACE: Prague
DATE: Spring 1540

To the two happy travellers, wherever this finds you,

Your last letter came by way of a Venetian glassmaker, a Swabian friar and a donkey, and it reached me with a tear in the page and a wine stain in the shape of Italy. I have kept it. I was a poor sergeant to you, lad, but I am a good friend, and a good friend wants to know if you are eating.

So you married her quietly in the cathedral with only the two of us as witnesses, and then you took her over the Alps. Vienna, Venice, Florence, Lyon. Does she make you stand in front of every fountain? Does the sword still hum? I notice you do not write of the sword. I notice you write of her.

I am glad. I mean it. I have seen what the road did to you. I have never been so glad of a wine stain.

One more thing, and then I will let you go. Her father's house on the hill has had lights in the windows at all hours this winter, and men I do not know go in and out. Her family keeps asking when you will come home. I did not tell them. Come back when you have had your fill of the world, not before.

Your friendt,
Niklas
